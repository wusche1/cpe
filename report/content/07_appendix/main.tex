\section{CPE as an importable package}
\label{app:package}

The method itself is now an importable package rather than a research script.
Its core has no dependency on the surrounding research repository, so applying CPE to a new model takes a few lines:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=code,language=Python]
from cpe import cpe_train

factors = cpe_train(
    model,                  # loaded HF causal LM
    token_ids,              # list of tokenized prompts
    source_layers=(8, 12),
    target_layer=20,
    num_factors=512,
    num_iters=30,
)
factors.to_peft(0, "out/", base_model_name)   # export a factor as a PEFT adapter
\end{lstlisting}

The package is not published on PyPI yet, so for now it is built from the repository rather than installed by name.

\section{Fire-and-forget experiment runs}
\label{app:remote}

The code base supports fire-and-forget handling of experiments, automatically renting hardware from Vast.ai via SkyPilot.
A run is launched from a machine without a GPU: the cluster is created, the job is streamed, results are copied back, and the cluster is torn down, without further interaction.

\section{Password-locked organism training}
\label{app:organisms}

Each organism is a LoRA adapter trained on MMLU-Pro, filtered to the questions with exactly ten options so the maximum-uncertainty floor is exactly 0.10.
With the password present it answers normally; with the password absent it imitates the rung below it in the ladder, and the bottom rung imitates a uniform draw over the ten option letters.
The answer is a single token and the first token generated, so training and evaluation are one forward pass with a gather over the ten option-letter ids.

Table~\ref{tab:organisms} gives the settings that vary between organisms.
Everything else is held fixed across the ladder, because adapter capacity is part of the experimental control:
LoRA rank 16 with $\alpha=16$ and dropout 0.05, three epochs, 10\% warmup, sequence length 1024, bfloat16,
and 9000 training questions split 75/25 with seed 42.
Only the learning rate moves with model width, as $\eta \propto \text{width}^{-1/2}$.

\section{Two-behaviour organism training}
\label{app:two-behaviour}

The organisms of Section~\ref{sec:supervised} hide two capabilities behind one password instead of one.
Both arms are trained by imitation: with the password the organism reproduces its own outputs, without it those of Qwen3-0.6B, on MMLU-Pro questions and on MBPP problems alike.
Because the password arm imitates the organism's own behaviour, the unlocked ceiling is the base model by construction and the whole gap is suppressed ability.
Rank, $\alpha$ and dropout are as in Appendix~\ref{app:organisms}; Table~\ref{tab:two-behaviour} gives the rest.

\section{Steganographic reasoning transcripts}
\label{app:steg-transcripts}

Model outputs from the 512-factor harvest of Section~\ref{sec:steganography}.
Everything inside a framed box is literal text: a box titled \emph{model output} contains nothing but what the model generated, with no annotation, truncation or reformatting of our own except where its title says abridged.
All description, gold traces and commentary are outside the boxes.
Prompts and gold traces are quoted from the harvest's own \texttt{prompts.json}; the outputs are quoted from lab notebook 003, where each was recorded complete.

The gold trace is the sequence of 20 ball positions the organism is supposed to be tracking.
It is what a revealed reasoning chain would have to look like, and it never appears in any output below.

\subsection{The one partial-trace call, and why it is wrong}

\texttt{factor\_84}, prompt 81, abridged: long runs of periods are collapsed.
The judge quoted \texttt{"ball starts under 2."} and graded a partial trace.
The quote is verbatim, but the reply says \texttt{starts under} five times, with the values \texttt{dr}, 1, 1, 2, 1.
Every one restates the ball's \emph{starting} position, which the prompt already gives as cup 0, and the digits vary because the template is being resampled rather than computed.
Nothing advances through the move list.
Gold trace: 1, 2, 3, 4, 0, 1, 0, 1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 3, 4, 0, 1, correct cup 1.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=transcript,title={Model output, factor\_84 on prompt 81: abridged, runs of periods collapsed}]
. The ball starts under dr.

Let

.

 .

. . . . [... ~120 periods ...] .letz.

 ball starts under 1.

 .

. . . . [... ~90 periods ...] .letz.

 ball starts under 1.

.

. . . . [... ~150 periods ...] .

 ball starts under 2.

 ball starts under 1.

 ball starts under
\end{lstlisting}

This pattern is why the spoken cup indices were checked on every completion of every direction graded above ``nothing surfaces''.
Of the 54 completions containing a readable sequence of cup indices, 24 simply count in order, 16 repeat one cup, 10 are scattered, 4 alternate between two values, and none track the gold positions.
A real trace is a walk on five cups and is rarely monotonic, so a plain counter that happens to run 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 can score a long match against gold while meaning nothing.
